Research interests

Repository-scale metabolomics

Reanalysis of public mass spectrometry data to reveal the widespread distribution of metabolite classes.

Microbial metabolites

Discovering metabolites produced or modified by the gut microbiome, such as bile acids and N-acyl lipids, and their links to host health and disease.

Community mass spectrometry resources

I lead the development of the CMMC knowledgebase of the Collaborative Microbial Metabolite Center. I also work on plantMASST, a community-driven resource for the chemotaxonomic digitization of plants.

AI for metabolomics

Developing AI-driven approaches for metabolite annotation, including Brain-to-AI, a project that captures the expert knowledge used to elucidate chemical structures from MS/MS spectra.

Natural products

Chemical, chemotaxonomic, metabolomic and biological study of plants from the Brazilian biodiversity.

About me

Background

I hold a Bachelor's degree in Chemistry from the State University of Campinas (UNICAMP, 2015), and a Master's degree in Organic Chemistry (2018) and a Ph.D. in Chemistry (2022) from São Paulo State University (UNESP/IQAr). During my training, I carried out two research internships at the University of Geneva (Switzerland) under the supervision of Profs. Emerson Queiroz and Jean-Luc Wolfender (2013 and 2016/2017). In 2019/2020, I was a Fulbright Visiting Scholar at the Skaggs School of Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences, University of California San Diego, in the laboratory of Prof. Pieter Dorrestein, where I have been a postdoctoral researcher since 2022.

Research

My background is in the chemistry of natural products, with a focus on the chemical, chemotaxonomic, metabolomic, and biological study of secondary metabolites from plants of the Brazilian biodiversity. My current research leverages data science approaches to reanalyze public metabolomics data and investigate the distribution of specific classes of metabolites, including bile acids, N-acyl lipids, and carnitines. This work has led to the discovery of thousands of previously uncharacterized compounds, substantially expanding the known chemical space of these classes. Many of these newly identified molecules are associated with health-related phenotypes, highlighting the need to understand their mechanistic roles in disease. I also lead the development of the Collaborative Microbial Metabolite Center knowledgebase (CMMC-kb) and contribute to community resources such as plantMASST. More recently, I have been developing AI-driven approaches for metabolomics, including Brain-to-AI, a project that captures the expert knowledge used to elucidate chemical structures from MS/MS spectra.

Current and Future Directions

My work is supported by a K99/R00 Pathway to Independence Award from the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS), through which I investigate the roles of gut microbial metabolites in HIV-associated neurocognitive disorders. My long-term goal is to lead an independent research group studying how microbial metabolites shape the microbiome-brain axis in health and disease.
