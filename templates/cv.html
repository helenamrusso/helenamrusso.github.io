Research Interests

My primary research interests center on LC-MS/MS-based untargeted metabolomics, computational tools, big data mining, and artificial intelligence (AI), with a strong emphasis on developing innovative analytical approaches to understand complex biological systems. During my doctoral research, I applied LC-MS/MS-based untargeted metabolomics to investigate plant metabolism, integrating chemotaxonomic analysis with natural product discovery to uncover unusual metabolites with bioactive roles. As a postdoctoral researcher in the Dorrestein Lab, I expanded my expertise into computational metabolomics, developing large-scale spectral libraries, mining public data repositories, and creating community-accessible resources to investigate both microbial and plant-derived metabolites. I also develop AI-driven approaches for metabolomics, including Brain-to-AI, a project that captures the expert knowledge used to elucidate chemical structures from MS/MS spectra. In my independent research program, I aim to integrate computational and experimental approaches to discover new molecules and elucidate their biological functions, with the overarching goal of defining how they shape human health.

Keywords: Untargeted LC-MS/MS metabolomics, data mining, artificial intelligence, machine learning, database development, structural elucidation, microbial metabolites, plant metabolites, multi-omics, natural products, host signaling, human health.

Projects

Computational Metabolomics and Resource Development

2022 – Present

Postdoctoral project; Dr. Pieter C. Dorrestein. Skaggs School of Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences, University of California San Diego

  • Development of spectral libraries from public metabolomics repositories: I created comprehensive MS/MS spectral libraries by searching fragmentation patterns in public metabolomics datasets. I applied this computational approach to three classes of metabolites, and this allowed the creation of spectral libraries containing >5,500 putative bile acids, 851 N-acyl lipids, and >2,500 carnitines, representing the largest publicly available resources for metabolomic annotation of these compound classes. These libraries guided targeted synthesis and structural confirmation of dozens of previously unreported molecules, significantly expanding knowledge of metabolic diversity within these chemical classes.
  • Microbial metabolite knowledgebase development: I lead the development of the Collaborative Microbial Metabolite Center knowledgebase (CMMC-kb), a resource that integrates spectral and structural data of microbial metabolites with biological information, including microbial producers, bioactivity, and biosynthesis. This comprehensive resource accelerates metabolite annotation and contextualization while enabling mechanistic investigation of microbiome function, advancing both molecular identification and biological interpretation of metabolomics results.
  • Plant metabolomics database development: I developed plantMASST, a reference database that integrates 41,727 taxonomically defined plant extracts from 3,568 species. This database provides a foundation for large-scale chemotaxonomic investigations, allowing researchers to trace metabolite distributions across plant species using public metabolomics data. plantMASST has already enabled the discovery of previously uncharacterized plant-derived peptides, demonstrating its value for advancing natural products research.
  • Development of open science tools: I developed MetaboApps, a suite of modular web applications integrated into the GNPS ecosystem. These web applications are designed to make complex data analysis in metabolomics more accessible to the scientific community, as they can be used not only by beginners or experienced data scientists, but also clinicians, microbiologists, among others.
  • AI for structural elucidation (Brain-to-AI): I lead Brain-to-AI, a project that captures the expert knowledge used to elucidate chemical structures from MS/MS spectra. Much of this reasoning is never formally documented, and instead lives in laboratory notebooks, in annotated spectra, and in the explanations experts give when asked how they reached a conclusion. We capture it from handwritten notes, annotated spectra, and recorded interviews with specialists, and turn it into a skill that enables an AI assistant to annotate molecules from MS/MS data, grade its confidence, and cite the source of every inference.

Chemical profiling of plant species for chemotaxonomic investigations

2018 – 2022

Doctoral thesis project; Dr. Vanderlan da Silva Bolzani. Institute of Chemistry, São Paulo State University

I conducted a chemotaxonomic investigation of the Malpighiaceae botanical family using computational metabolomics tools to correlate classes of compounds with evolutionary patterns. During this work, I also applied LC-MS/MS to guide the isolation of unusual nitropropanoyl glucosides from plants, which showed larvicidal activity against Aedes aegypti larvae.

Phytochemical investigation of Niedenzuella multiglandulosa

2015 – 2018

Master's thesis project; Dr. Vanderlan da Silva Bolzani and Dr. Emerson Ferreira Queiroz. Institute of Chemistry, São Paulo State University

I performed the first phytochemical investigation of Niedenzuella multiglandulosa, an endemic Brazilian plant toxic to cattle. My research involved isolating and structurally characterizing the natural products accumulated in the leaves using NMR and LC-MS/MS.
